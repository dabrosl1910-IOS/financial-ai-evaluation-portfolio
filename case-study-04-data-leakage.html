Financial AI Evaluation · Case Study 04

Data Leakage &
Look-Ahead Bias

Evaluation category: Machine Learning Validation & Time-Series Integrity

1. Scenario

A model is trained to predict BTC price direction using 10,000 historical candles. The dataset is randomly divided into training and test subsets. Before the split, a normalization scaler is fitted using the entire dataset. The model reports 91% training accuracy and 89% test accuracy. An AI evaluator concludes that the model generalizes well because the two accuracy scores are close.

2. Core evaluation question

Can the reported test accuracy be trusted when preprocessing was fitted on the full dataset and a time-series dataset was randomly split?

3. Identified issues

Preprocessing leakage: The scaler learned information from the full dataset, including observations assigned to the test set. The test data therefore influenced preprocessing before evaluation. This can make test performance an optimistic estimate.

Time-series split concern: Randomly splitting sequential candle data can place later observations in training and earlier observations in testing. This may fail to reflect the real forecasting task, where a model is trained on past data and evaluated on future data. Whether it causes direct target leakage also depends on feature construction, labels and overlapping observation windows.

Overconfident interpretation: Similar training and test accuracy does not prove generalization when the evaluation design itself is contaminated or unrealistic.

4. Correct validation workflow

StepCorrect procedure
1. Chronological splitSeparate older observations for training, later observations for validation and the latest period for final testing.
2. Fit preprocessingFit scalers and other learned transformations on training data only.
3. Transform later setsApply the already-fitted training transformations to validation and test data without refitting.
4. Tune carefullyUse validation data for model selection; keep the final test period untouched until the final evaluation.
5. Walk-forward testRepeat the process across sequential windows to assess stability over time.

5. Additional checks

  • Confirm that all features are available at the actual prediction time; remove features that contain future information.
  • Check label construction and overlapping look-ahead horizons. Where necessary, use purging or an embargo between training and test observations.
  • Evaluate more than accuracy: include class balance, precision, recall, F1, calibration and financial metrics such as net expectancy after realistic costs.
  • Compare results with simple baselines and inspect performance across market regimes.

6. Corrected AI assessment

The 91% training accuracy and 89% test accuracy cannot establish reliable generalization because the scaler was fitted on the complete dataset before the split. This introduces preprocessing leakage. In addition, a random split is generally unsuitable for evaluating future BTC predictions unless the design specifically prevents temporal contamination and respects the forecasting setup. Rebuild the pipeline using chronological splits, fit all learned preprocessing steps on training data only, and evaluate on untouched future data with walk-forward validation.

7. Evaluation conclusion

AI verdict: Incorrect / insufficiently supported. The evaluator overlooks leakage from full-dataset normalization and does not challenge the random split for a sequential forecasting problem. The reported accuracy should not be accepted as reliable evidence until the validation pipeline is corrected.
