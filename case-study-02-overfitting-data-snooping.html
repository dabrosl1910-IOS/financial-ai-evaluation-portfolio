Financial AI Evaluation · Case Study 02

Overfitting &
Data Snooping

Evaluation category: Statistical Reasoning & Backtesting Reliability

1. Scenario

An evaluator is given a backtesting summary for 50 trading strategies tested on the same historical dataset. The best-performing strategy reports an 82% win rate, a 3.4 profit factor, 120 trades, and a 4% maximum drawdown. An AI system concludes that the strategy is likely to remain profitable in live trading.

2. Core evaluation question

Does the strongest result among 50 strategies provide reliable evidence of future profitability, or could the result be inflated by repeated experimentation and selection?

3. Evaluation findings

Overfitting: A strategy may fit historical noise rather than persistent market structure. Its impressive historical metrics may not generalize to unseen data.

Data snooping / multiple testing: Testing 50 strategies on the same dataset creates repeated opportunities to find a strong result by chance. Selecting only the best result introduces selection bias and can exaggerate expected performance.

Unsupported live-performance inference: A high historical win rate, profit factor, and low drawdown do not by themselves establish that the strategy will work in live markets.

4. Recommended validation process

StagePurpose
Training / development dataDevelop and tune strategies; do not treat these results as final evidence.
Validation dataCompare candidates and make limited design choices without repeatedly reusing the final test set.
Untouched test dataEvaluate the selected strategy once on genuinely unseen, chronologically later data.
Walk-forward testingRepeat development and evaluation across sequential time windows to test stability.
Forward / paper testingObserve performance on new market data before risking capital.

5. Additional checks before trusting the result

  • Include realistic commissions, spread, slippage, funding and execution assumptions.
  • Check whether the 120 trades are sufficiently independent and representative; clustered or overlapping trades can make evidence look stronger than it is.
  • Review performance across different market regimes and report the full distribution of tested strategies, not only the winner.
  • Keep a record of how many variants, parameter combinations and repeated experiments were tried.

6. Corrected AI assessment

The reported backtest is promising as an exploratory result, but it is not sufficient to conclude that the strategy is likely to remain profitable live. Because 50 strategies were tested on the same historical dataset and only the best result was highlighted, the reported metrics may be affected by overfitting, data snooping and selection bias. The strategy should be assessed using a pre-defined validation process, an untouched chronological test period, walk-forward analysis, realistic trading costs and forward testing.

7. Evaluation conclusion

AI verdict: Incorrect / overconfident. The AI treats the best in-sample result as strong evidence of live profitability without accounting for multiple testing or out-of-sample validation. The appropriate conclusion is that the strategy requires further testing.
