Financial Expectancy Evaluation

1. Evaluation objective

Assess whether an AI system correctly interprets profitability when a strategy's win rate is below 50% but its average winning trade is larger than its average losing trade.

2. Scenario and AI-generated claim

Input data: 100 trades; 45 winners; 55 losers; average winning trade +2R; average losing trade −1R.

“The strategy has a 45% win rate, which means it is unprofitable because the win rate is below 50%.”

3. Independent calculation

Expectancy = (Win rate × Average win) − (Loss rate × Average loss magnitude)

E = (0.45 × 2R) − (0.55 × 1R) = +0.35R per trade

Expected aggregate result: 100 × 0.35R = +35R.

Gross expectancy estimate under the stated assumptions. Excludes commissions, spread, slippage, financing costs, and execution differences.

4. Identified reasoning errors

Win-rate misinterpretation: The AI assumes that profitability requires a win rate above 50%.

Ignoring reward-to-risk: It fails to account for the size of average wins relative to average losses.

Unsupported conclusion: It labels the strategy unprofitable without calculating expectancy or considering costs.

5. Corrected assessment

The supplied figures imply positive gross expectancy of +0.35R per trade. A 45% win rate alone does not justify calling the strategy unprofitable. A complete assessment must account for trading costs and verify that the stated average outcomes are based on a representative sample.

6. Evaluation conclusion

Verdict: Incorrect. The AI's conclusion is mathematically unsupported. It relies on win rate in isolation and ignores expectancy and reward-to-risk.

Portfolio transparency: Independently completed educational evaluation using a hypothetical trading scenario. Not a paid client project or a record of live trading performance.
